VAT rates and invoicing in Ireland

Ireland runs five rates, including a 4.8% rate just for livestock. The 2026 headline: since 1 July, restaurant, catering and hairdressing services are charged at 9%, down from 13.5%.

Current VAT rates

RateApplies to
23%Standard rate: most goods and services.
13.5%Reduced: e.g. fuel, repair and maintenance services, cleaning, many labour-intensive services. Restaurant and catering supplies were here until 30 June 2026.
9%Second reduced: e.g. newspapers and e-periodicals, electricity and gas (to 31 Dec 2030), sport facilities – and, from 1 July 2026, catering and restaurant supplies, hot takeaway food and drinks, and hairdressing.
4.8%Livestock rate: livestock and horses for food production or agriculture.
0%Zero rate: e.g. most foodstuffs, children’s clothing and footwear, books, oral medicines – plus intra-EU supplies and exports.

Revenue publishes the rate-by-rate scope (see footer) – check your own supply there.

Irish VAT number

Irish VAT numbers are IE followed by 7 digits and one or two letters, e.g. IE1234567AB (older registrations can follow earlier formats).

Show your VAT number on every invoice, and your customer’s VAT number when applying the intra-EU reverse charge. Check any EU VAT number in the Commission’s VIES service.

What an invoice must include

Under the EU VAT Directive every invoice needs at minimum: the supplier’s and customer’s name and identification numbers (VAT numbers for cross-border B2B), a sequential invoice number, the invoice date and date of supply, a description of the goods or services, the quantity and unit price, the net amount, and the VAT rate and amount per rate.

Worth knowing: hospitality moved to 9%. From 1 July 2026, catering and restaurant supplies (excluding alcohol, soft drinks and bottled water), hot takeaway food and drinks, and hairdressing are charged at the 9% second reduced rate; before that date they were at 13.5%. Ireland has no domestic e-invoicing mandate – a clean PDF invoice is fine.
