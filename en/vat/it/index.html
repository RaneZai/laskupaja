VAT rates and invoicing in Italy

Italy (IVA) has a 22% standard rate plus three reduced tiers – and the EU’s longest-running e-invoicing mandate: domestic invoices have been digital-only since 2019.

Current VAT rates

RateApplies to
22%Standard rate: most goods and services.
10%Reduced: e.g. domestic electricity and gas, medicines, building-renovation work.
5%Further reduced: e.g. certain foodstuffs and social/health services.
4%Super-reduced: basic foodstuffs, beverages and agricultural products, and books.
0%Intra-EU supplies and exports, with both parties’ VAT numbers on the invoice.

The reduced-rate lists live in Tabella A of DPR 633/1972 – check your own supply there.

Italian VAT number (Partita IVA)

The partita IVA used in EU trade is IT + 11 digits, e.g. IT12345678901.

Show your VAT number on every invoice, and your customer’s VAT number when applying the intra-EU reverse charge. Check any EU VAT number in the Commission’s VIES service.

What an invoice must include

Under the EU VAT Directive every invoice needs at minimum: the supplier’s and customer’s name and identification numbers (VAT numbers for cross-border B2B), a sequential invoice number, the invoice date and date of supply, a description of the goods or services, the quantity and unit price, the net amount, and the VAT rate and amount per rate.

Worth knowing: the SDI mandate. Since 1 January 2019, invoices between businesses established in Italy must be electronic (FatturaPA XML) and transmitted through the tax agency’s Exchange System (Sistema di Interscambio, SDI) – an invoice not sent through SDI counts as never issued. The duty applies to domestic Italian flows; a foreign supplier invoicing an Italian client from abroad is outside SDI.
