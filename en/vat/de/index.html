VAT rates and invoicing in Germany

Germany (Umsatzsteuer, USt) has the EU’s best-known rate pair: a 19% standard rate and one reduced rate of 7%. If you sell to German businesses, here is what belongs on the invoice.

Current VAT rates

RateApplies to
19%Standard rate: most goods and services.
7%Reduced rate: e.g. most foodstuffs, books, passenger transport, hotel accommodation, cultural events.
0%No domestic zero rate – 0% appears on intra-EU supplies and exports, with both parties’ VAT numbers on the invoice.

Always check the scope for your own supply – §12 UStG and its annexes define the reduced-rate goods.

German VAT number (USt-IdNr.)

The Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer is the country code DE followed by nine digits, e.g. DE123456789.

Domestic German invoices often carry the tax office number (Steuernummer) instead; for cross-border B2B trade the USt-IdNr. is what counts. Show your own VAT number on every invoice you issue, and your customer’s VAT number when you apply the intra-EU reverse charge. You can check any EU VAT number in the Commission’s VIES service.

What an invoice must include

Under the EU VAT Directive every invoice needs at minimum: the supplier’s and customer’s name and identification numbers (VAT numbers for cross-border B2B), a sequential invoice number, the invoice date and date of supply, a description of the goods or services, the quantity and unit price, the net amount, and the VAT rate and amount per rate.

Worth knowing: E-Rechnung. Since 1 January 2025 every German business must be able to receive electronic B2B invoices in the EN 16931 format (XRechnung/ZUGFeRD) – without agreeing to it in advance. The duty to issue e-invoices phases in afterwards, so a clean PDF invoice remains fine for most small issuers for now, but plan for structured e-invoices.
