VAT rates and invoicing in the Netherlands

The Netherlands (btw) keeps it simple: one standard rate and one reduced rate – but note the 2026 change that moved accommodation from 9% to 21%.

Current VAT rates

RateApplies to
21%General rate: everything not reduced, zero-rated or exempt – and, since 1 January 2026, also accommodation (logies).
9%Reduced rate: e.g. foodstuffs and medicines, books, passenger transport, hairdressing. Standalone facilities sold with accommodation (e.g. breakfast) stay at 9%.
0%Intra-EU supplies, exports and a few other operations – with the VAT numbers of both parties on the invoice.

Some sectors use flat rates (e.g. sports canteens); the Belastingdienst site lists the full scope.

Dutch VAT number (btw-nummer)

The VAT number is NL + 9 digits + the letter B + two digits, e.g. NL123456789B01.

Show your VAT number on every invoice, and your customer’s VAT number when applying the intra-EU reverse charge. Check any EU VAT number in the Commission’s VIES service.

What an invoice must include

Under the EU VAT Directive every invoice needs at minimum: the supplier’s and customer’s name and identification numbers (VAT numbers for cross-border B2B), a sequential invoice number, the invoice date and date of supply, a description of the goods or services, the quantity and unit price, the net amount, and the VAT rate and amount per rate.

Worth knowing: accommodation moved to 21%. From 1 January 2026, lodging and its accompanying facilities are taxed at the general 21% rate instead of the reduced 9%. If you invoice stays in the Netherlands, update your rate – facilities sold separately (breakfast, pool access) remain at 9%. The Netherlands has no domestic e-invoicing mandate for B2B.

Currency

The Netherlands’ currency is the euro (EUR) – the generator’s totals work as-is.
