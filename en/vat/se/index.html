VAT rates and invoicing in Sweden

Sweden (moms) has one of the EU’s highest standard rates at 25%, with two reduced tiers and a short list of tax-free sectors. Amounts are in Swedish krona (SEK).

Current VAT rates

RateApplies to
25%General rate: most goods and services.
12%Reduced: e.g. foodstuffs, hotel accommodation, restaurant and catering services.
6%Further reduced: e.g. books and newspapers, passenger transport, cultural and sporting events, museum admissions.
Tax-freeOutside VAT altogether: e.g. health and medical care, dental care and social services (no VAT is charged or shown).

Skatteverket’s momssatser page (footer) lists the full scope and exceptions – check it for your own supply.

Swedish VAT number

The momsregistreringsnummer is SE + the 10-digit organisation number + 01, e.g. SE556123456701.

Show your VAT number on every invoice, and your customer’s VAT number when applying the intra-EU reverse charge. Check any EU VAT number in the Commission’s VIES service.

What an invoice must include

Under the EU VAT Directive every invoice needs at minimum: the supplier’s and customer’s name and identification numbers (VAT numbers for cross-border B2B), a sequential invoice number, the invoice date and date of supply, a description of the goods or services, the quantity and unit price, the net amount, and the VAT rate and amount per rate.

Worth knowing: e-invoices in public procurement. Since 1 April 2019 the Swedish e-invoicing act (e-fakturalagen) requires suppliers invoicing the Swedish public sector under the procurement acts to send e-invoices in the European standard (usually exchanged via Peppol), and contracting authorities to receive them. Private-sector B2B invoicing has no such mandate – a clean PDF invoice is fine.
